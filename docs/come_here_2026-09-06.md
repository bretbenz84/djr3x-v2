Owner report: R3X faced Bret, who was visible and asked "Come here." He turned away,
searched around the room, and claimed he would clip something behind him despite
at least five feet of obstacle clearance. Bret's prior four-mic array bench test
produced accurate directions. There was no spoken behind/side instruction.

Sources: `logs/conversation-2026-09-06-00-44-36.log` and
`logs/djr3x-2026-09-06-00-44-36.log`.

## Evidence and causes

- **00:45:52:** voice and face agreed on Bret, voice score .816.
- **00:46:05–10:** the .81-second voiced command had weak voice scores: T'Joy .475,
  Bret .426, margin .050 below .070. Identity appropriately abstained; continuity
  proposed Bret but could not authorize identity/learning. The visible face list
  still contained Bret. Unknown identity was being treated as a reason to search.
- **00:46:05:** an eager decode had just exceeded its three-second wait. The direction
  read selected +106°. `_accumulate_speech` copied audio BEFORE waiting for that
  decode, while its caller took the direction window's end AFTER the wait and its
  start from VAD onset, omitting audio pre-roll. The two evidence streams could cover
  different intervals. This is a confirmed code defect; the old log does not retain
  enough separate register samples to reconstruct the correct bearing for this audio.
- **00:46:10:** `request_come_here` issued +105.84° immediately from the voice bearing
  without checking the camera. Even a correctly identified, already-visible caller
  could be turned away from by this path. Camera-first logic existed only in later ticks.
- **00:46:19 onward:** radar-directed turns of approximately −179°, −130°, −36°,
  and +98°, plus compass corrections, explain the apparent random spinning.
- **00:46:30:** `ring@0->fr@0.56m` limited the turn (0.56 m is distance from the
  axle pivot, not raw sensor clearance). The forward-escape check read 0.07 m.
  The radar turn's refusal fell through to a sweep refusal in the same tick; one
  second later the still-active request sent the radar turn anyway. The fixed
  spoken line claimed an obstacle behind him even though the limiter was front-right.

## Changes

Direction and interval visual evidence are now sampled against the copied audio's
duration and capture endpoint, before any eager-decode wait. Diagnostic samples
retain both `DOA_VALUE` and beam azimuth: the old field labeled "chip" could actually
be a substituted beam angle. No array calibration or microphone DSP setting changed.

Request startup checks the camera first and takes head ownership immediately. A
short weak voice can bind movement to the sole visible recent conversational partner
using the existing short-clip score/continuity limits; mixed/strong conflicting voice,
conflicting interval visual/direction evidence, stale continuity, or multiple visible
people prevent that fallback. The identity verdict remains ambiguous and cannot grow
voiceprints or authorize personal memory writes. A known off-camera caller is never
replaced by the visible partner.

Once acquired at startup, the person's ID stays bound to the errand and the opening
voice prior is discarded. A centered measured face starts a straight approach. Stale
head locks on missing faces and missing face-box geometry do not permit forward driving.
Refused turns terminate the request. Persistent front blockage or missing geometry
has an eight-second wait and an explanation instead of waiting indefinitely while
the visible face continually resets the search timeout.

The turn refusal now reports a sensor-detected turn-path obstruction without inventing
its location. Refusals and blocked approaches log the full ToF dictionary, including
separate radial readings when firmware publishes them. Swing geometry, forward escape,
and firmware obstacle stops remain in effect.

## Verification and remaining uncertainty

`tests/test_come_here_regression.py` exercises the production attribution-to-motion
handoff with the logged voice scores, camera-before-voice priority, target binding,
off-camera callers, conflicting evidence, stale locks, missing geometry, front-block
timeout, and refusal cancellation. Its capture test simulates three seconds of decode
delay with a different sound arriving during the delay: the caller's pre-roll bearing
is retained and the later sound is excluded. Tests run through
`tools/run_lean_checks.py` with fake movement and speech and real I/O blocked.

These tests establish software behavior, not physical arrival accuracy. No live robot
run, servo movement, mic recording, DSP write, or firmware flash was performed.
The logs prove that the software received near-obstacle readings; they do not establish
what produced them. With the owner's reported clearance, sensor self-returns, fusion,
or another sensing defect still need evidence. The new raw diagnostics are intended
to distinguish those cases in the next owner-run test, rather than disabling protection.

The large left turn is traceable to the wake reflex, not an unexplained firmware
maneuver. The old camera guard only held when the audio bearing agreed with the
visible face. Thus contradictory audio could make the reflex look away from someone
already on camera. Radar agreement did not establish that the return was Bret.
This identifies a software decision failure; it does not prove the microphone array
itself was inaccurate, nor establish the source of the conflicting bearing.

Two changes address this sequence:

- Process the drive result before post-drive alignment or search, including when
  the firmware state has returned to `blocked`. A completed drive holds position
  when the caller is near or absent. A far-looking face permits a bounded retry
  only with independent clear front range. Older firmware without split radial
  telemetry now holds instead of retrying from face size alone; that can leave an
  approach short when a sensor falsely completes it, but cannot send R3X searching
  or realigning beside the caller. An in-flight drive is also left alone.
- Bare wake calls preserve a visible person and cannot take over an active approach.
  Recheck this guard after waiting for the base to become idle, immediately before
  dispatching a turn. Explicit directional invitations and off-camera wake calls
  retain their orientation paths.

Regression cases reproduce completion with a 13° face offset, a dipped/lost camera,
blocked front state and the logged unsplit ranges, plus the +91° wake/+99° radar
conflict. They also verify explicit `over here` behavior and a face appearing during
the wake worker's base-idle wait. Offline checks use the same isolated runner;
physical behavior still needs an owner-run floor test.

The persistent 72 mm front-right reading remains unexplained. It caused the refusal
and the log also records a near-front flinch. Arrival/wake fixes do not bypass those
obstacle protections or change sensor calibration.

## Owner clarification: slightly right at the wake call

Bret confirmed that at "Hey Rex" R3X was already pointed toward him, with Bret
slightly to R3X's right. The expected direction was near forward/right; +91° left
was wrong. Identifying the wake reflex explained the command's source, not why its
direction estimate was wrong.

The logged +91° group comprised 16 polls averaging 0.02M speech energy. The two
other groups were single polls at −89°/0.12M and −29°/0.10M. The runtime accepted
low-energy, speech-flagged `DOA_VALUE` polls below its existing 0.05M beam-energy
floor, then summed their weighted votes over a 2.5 s wake lookback. A persistent weak
direction could therefore outweigh stronger but fewer polls and pass the motion
count/share checks. This differs from simply reading the direction register while
the owner talks during the stationary array test. The measured angle convention and
mount calibration were not changed.

Now only polls at or above the existing speech-energy floor can vote when that
register is available, and a chosen group needs at least three agreeing polls.
Measured zero energy cannot fall back to unweighted direction counting; an unavailable
energy register is represented separately as `None`. Radar can consider only eligible
groups. If it promotes another group, movement checks use that group's own sample
count and share. An unusable wake clears an older stored bearing.

`tests/test_voice_direction_quality.py` includes a reconstruction consistent with
the logged group averages: sixteen 0.02M left polls plus the two stronger, disagreeing
polls now yield no direction. A supported −15° caller beats repeated weak +91° polls,
even with radar at +99°. The original wake's individual samples were not logged, so
this is not an exact replay or proof that −29° was Bret. Wake diagnostics now retain
the full sampled window, register/beam directions and rejected evidence as well as
successful choices. Camera priority continues to protect the actual visible caller.

## Follow-up run: recognized Bret but kept searching (01:30 log)

Sources: `logs/conversation-2026-09-06-01-30-18.log` and
`logs/djr3x-2026-09-06-01-30-18.log`. The owner confirmed that Jeremy Thomas was
not in the room or speaking. His name below is a false recognizer label.

| Time | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| 01:31:23 | Bret's two-word command has only 0.51 s voiced audio. Voice scores: Jeremy .608 (one print), Bret .449 (eight). The configured general threshold is .50; the resolver incorrectly certifies Jeremy as "strong voice." Direction evidence abstains. |
| 01:31:24 | Movement starts searching specifically for person 4, Jeremy. |
| 01:31:25, 01:31:35 | Radar turns +18.3° and +75.1°, each followed by compass corrections. |
| 01:31:41 | Camera identifies Bret, person 1. Motion logs "seeing person 1 but the requester is 4 — continuing the search." |
| 01:31:45 | Despite Bret remaining visible, motion sends +155.0° toward another radar return. |
| 01:31:49 | An independent recognition greeting addresses Bret by name. It did not change the movement target. |
| 01:31:55, 01:32:03 | Further +71.8° and −95.7° searches. |
| 01:32:09 | Search expires. No forward `come` was issued in this request. |

The earlier camera-priority fixes assumed a correctly identified caller, or an
ambiguous caller whose face was already visible at request time. This run exposed
both gaps: a wrong supposedly strong voice label and a face found later. It also
exposed an architectural problem: losing a face after finding it could reopen the
old search branches. Several old unit tests explicitly required that behavior.

The changes now enforce the complete request lifecycle:

1. **Do not certify a different speaker from a brief weak match.** A new speaker
   needs more than a sub-second clip below .70 unless interval visual evidence
   corroborates them. Such a clip can remain unidentified while still commanding
   movement; it cannot authorize learning or a confident personal label.
2. **Retain evidence until acquisition.** A sole visible recent partner can resolve
   the movement target for that unconfirmed brief switch when their current voice
   score supports them and interval evidence does not conflict. The .608/.449 case
   can acquire Bret later instead of rejecting him for not being Jeremy. Longer or
   stronger other callers, stale context, conflicting evidence and multiple visible
   faces prevent this fallback. Voice profiles and databases were not changed.
3. **Latch acquisition.** First camera acquisition stops a running scan and ends
   search for this request. A face dropout cannot restart radar, voice-bearing or
   blind search turns. Queued search dispatch and competing orientation reflexes
   respect this state. The target cannot switch to another face after acquisition.
4. **Align and approach.** Measured camera alignment is capped at 30° and three
   attempts. If parking the neck temporarily loses a side-on face, its observed
   world bearing permits bounded alignment while waiting to see the same caller
   again. Other losses hold position; after eight seconds the request ends with
   an explanation. A visible aligned caller starts the obstacle-gated approach.
5. **Remove delayed search motion.** Come turns do not queue compass corrections
   or swing escapes. Camera feedback owns their next heading. Firmware yaw control
   and obstacle/swing checks still apply, and ordinary explicit turns retain their
   existing verification behavior.

`tests/test_come_target_acquisition.py` exercises the production attribution-to-motion
handoff with the field scores, followed by scanning, Bret appearing, a forward approach
and a camera dropout. It also covers mid-turn stopping, delayed search/reflex rejection,
side-on neck recentering, bounded alignment and valid other callers. These are software
checks, not a claim of physical arrival accuracy; an owner-run floor test remains needed.
